\subsection{Defect-dependent Li adsorption}

The five structures create a wide range of local Li binding strengths
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}a). Across the 15 PBE-D3(BJ)
fixed-geometry calculations, the adsorption energies span -2.967 to -0.875 eV per Li.
The best sampled values are -0.918, -2.967, -2.301, -1.587, and -2.465 eV for
pristine, monovacancy, divacancy, Stone--Wales, and Si$_4$--graphene,
respectively. Thus, the monovacancy gives the strongest sampled adsorption,
followed by the Si$_4$ motif and divacancy.

The family trend is robust to the tested placement. All 12 non-pristine
energies are more negative than all three pristine energies. Even the least
favorable non-pristine placement, Stone--Wales at -1.474 eV, is 0.555 eV
more favorable than the best pristine placement at -0.918 eV. PBE-D3(BJ) also
retains the preceding PBE rank order of the three sites in every family. The
within-family spans range from 0.044 eV for pristine graphene to 0.906 eV for
the monovacancy, so absolute adsorption strengths remain site-sensitive even
though the defect-versus-pristine separation is unchanged.

The strong monovacancy values are consistent with the chemically intuitive role
of under-coordinated and reconstructed carbon near a vacancy, while the Si$_4$
motif introduces direct Li--Si and Si--C environments absent from pristine
graphene. The divacancy produces an intermediate response, showing that the
removal of carbon atoms alone does not determine the adsorption strength; the
result depends on the resulting local topology. The weaker but consistently
negative Stone--Wales values show that topology also changes the magnitude of
the response. Complete site-resolved values are provided in the Supporting
Information.

These adsorption energies compare three current Li placements per family and
therefore establish local energetic reference values rather than fully optimized adsorption
thermodynamics. In particular, they do not include an exhaustive relaxed-site
search, Li--Li clustering, multilayer graphite, or electrode--electrolyte
effects. Within this defined fixed-geometry comparison, however, the 2.092 eV
span directly demonstrates that the five local environments probe substantially
different Li chemistry.

The isolated-Li reference is common to every adsorption-energy row, so it
cancels from the between-family differences used here. The absolute adsorption
energies are not lithiation voltages or formation energies relative to metallic
Li; those quantities require a bulk-Li chemical potential and explicit coverage
sampling.

\subsection{Fine-tuning improves interpolation within the sampled configurational domain}

The 273-frame DFT label set spans all five structural families. On the original
correlated test split, the MACE-MPA-0 foundation model gives an all-family
force RMSE of 285.2 meV \AA$^{-1}$, whereas the first fine-tuned model gives
20.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$. Thus, target-domain fine-tuning reduces the force error by
92.9\% for configurations drawn from the same relaxation and site/path sampling
domain.

This comparison establishes a real interpolation improvement, but it is not the
transferability test used for the central conclusion. Images from the same fixed
interpolation paths were correlated across the original train, validation, and
test partitions. We therefore rebuilt the data split by keeping each path group
intact and corrected the elemental energy baseline through foundation-assisted
$E_0$ estimation. The resulting grouped test set contains one configuration per
family and is too small for population-level error estimates. Full split
definitions, family-resolved interpolation errors, $E_0$ fitting details, and
three-seed sensitivity results are reported in the Supporting Information. A
more stringent assessment is provided by the direct DFT test on displaced
configurations described next.

\subsection{A family-balanced DFT benchmark shows broad local improvement}

The 25-configuration benchmark compares model behavior on family-balanced,
post-training perturbations with the small correlation-controlled grouped split
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}b). All 25 PBE single-point calculations
converged electronically. Across the complete benchmark, the pooled
force RMSE decreases from 450.0 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for MACE-MPA-0 to
267.5 meV \AA$^{-1}$ after grouped-$E_0$ fine-tuning, a 40.6\% reduction. The
family-resolved force RMSE decreases in every environment: from 484.7 to 275.4
meV \AA$^{-1}$ for pristine graphene, 449.7 to 256.3 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the
monovacancy, 381.3 to 246.3 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the divacancy, 496.3 to 297.5
meV \AA$^{-1}$ for Stone--Wales graphene, and 427.5 to 258.5 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for
the Si$_4$ motif. At the individual-configuration level, the fine-tuned force
RMSE is lower for 23 of 25 predefined perturbations.

The component-resolved comparison localizes the remaining error. Fine-tuning
reduces the Li-force RMSE from 992.6 to 65.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$, whereas the
substrate-force RMSE changes from 432.3 to 269.9 meV \AA$^{-1}$. The residual
balanced-test error is therefore dominated by forces on the substrate network,
consistent with the more severe reconstructed-carbon challenge examined below.

Because the benchmark was defined without model predictions and weights all five
families equally, it probes a broader local domain than the
one-configuration-per-family grouped test. It nevertheless establishes
robustness only around the five converged structures, not global transferability.
Residual family errors remain 246--297 meV \AA$^{-1}$, and the largest
fine-tuned frame error is 484 meV \AA$^{-1}$. These values are substantially
larger than the correlated interpolation baseline and motivate the separate
high-displacement comparison below.

The displacement-amplitude dependence makes this boundary explicit
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}c). For
coordinate perturbation standard deviations of 0.03, 0.06, and 0.10 \AA{}, the
fine-tuned pooled force RMSE is 79.2, 190.1, and 373.6 meV \AA$^{-1}$,
respectively. The corresponding MACE-MPA-0 values are 475.8, 435.6, and
450.8 meV \AA$^{-1}$, so the relative reduction narrows from 83.4\% near the
relaxed structures to 17.1\% at the largest perturbation. Fine-tuning therefore
improves every sampled family, but its advantage decreases as configurations
move farther from the local reference structures.

\subsection{Transferability is local-environment dependent}

The nine selected finite-temperature snapshots provide an explicit test outside
the small-displacement training domain (Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}d).
For the unfine-tuned foundation model, the frame-level force RMSE is
1133 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the three monovacancy snapshots and
310 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the six Si$_4$--graphene snapshots. The grouped-$E_0$
fine-tuned model reduces the Si$_4$ error to 113 meV \AA$^{-1}$, a 63\%
reduction, but changes the monovacancy error only to
1097 meV \AA$^{-1}$, a 3\% reduction. Across all nine frames, the corresponding
RMSE changes from 701 to 640 meV \AA$^{-1}$.

This contrast is the central transferability result. Fine-tuning is effective
for the selected Si-containing configurations but does not supply the force
physics required for the reconstructed monovacancy configurations. The
monovacancy snapshots contain minimum C--C distances of 1.204--1.217 \AA{}, much
shorter than an equilibrium graphitic C--C bond, whereas the selected Si$_4$
snapshots retain minimum C--C distances near 1.36 \AA{} and include Si--Si
contacts down to 1.994 \AA{}. The large monovacancy force errors therefore align
with a specific structural regime: highly strained local carbon reconstruction.
They are not explained by a uniform loss of accuracy across all displaced
configurations.

Aggregate interpolation metrics would obscure this difference. The
correlated-split 20.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$ force RMSE is more than an order of magnitude
smaller than either high-displacement family error, yet the two families respond
very differently to corrected fine-tuning. For active learning, the appropriate
next data are therefore not additional near-equilibrium frames sampled in the
same proportions. They are DFT configurations that resolve strained C--C
reconstruction and Li coordination near monovacancies, together with
family-balanced displaced configurations that test whether the observed
contrast persists beyond the present nine frames. The present DFT-validated
snapshots identify that next sampling target but have not been incorporated into
a further training cycle; the work therefore does not constitute a closed
concurrent-learning loop.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

The present evidence supports two linked conclusions: local defect chemistry
changes dilute Li adsorption, and local environment also controls the
extrapolative reliability of the fine-tuned MLIP. The numerical adsorption
values remain fixed-geometry single-point calculations. The balanced force benchmark
contains five perturbations per family, while the high-displacement comparison
contains three monovacancy and six Si$_4$ frames. The
Si$_4$--graphene structure is a minimal local motif rather than a representative
composite anode. Fixed interpolation paths and finite-window trajectories are
included in the Supporting Information as methodological context; they are not
used to infer migration barriers or diffusion coefficients. These boundaries do
not alter the observed contrast between the two snapshot subsets, but they set
the scale of the conclusions and motivate broader, family-balanced DFT sampling.
