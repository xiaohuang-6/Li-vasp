# Response To Reviewers

We thank the reviewers for their careful and constructive assessment. The revised manuscript has been substantially narrowed so that the claims match the evidence now available. In particular, we no longer present fixed-geometry path scans as Li migration barriers, no longer claim converged diffusion coefficients or practical anode performance, and no longer interpret short molecular dynamics trajectories as definitive transport mechanisms. The manuscript now presents a validation-first DFT--MACE workflow that combines local Li--C--Si adsorption-energy screening, leakage-aware model evaluation, and direct DFT stress tests.

## Comment 1: Fixed-Geometry Path Scans Were Misidentified As Migration Barriers

We agree. The original path-span values were endpoint/path-roughness descriptors from frozen Cartesian interpolation scans, not relaxed minimum-energy-path barriers. In several cases, the reported path span equaled the endpoint energy difference, demonstrating that the maximum and minimum occurred at the endpoints rather than at an independent saddle point.

The revised manuscript removes migration-barrier claims from the abstract, results, tables, and conclusion. The relevant section is now titled "Endpoint energy differences, not migration barriers", and Table 3 reports the quantities as fixed-geometry path-scan descriptors. We explicitly state that the pristine graphene and Stone--Wales values cannot be used to claim low Li migration barriers, and that meaningful kinetic comparison requires relaxed CI-NEB paths benchmarked against prior graphene literature.

## Comment 2: Missing Test-Set Error, Foundation-Model Baseline, And Uncertainty Diagnostics

We agree. The revised manuscript now reports same-workflow held-out test-set errors and a direct comparison with the unfine-tuned MACE-MPA-0 foundation model. Fine-tuning reduces the all-family held-out force RMSE from 285.2 to 20.1 meV A^-1. The all-family energy RMSE remains 39.1 meV atom^-1, mainly due to a systematic Si4--graphene family offset, and this limitation is now discussed explicitly. The text no longer treats this split as an independent transferability benchmark. A grouped split with foundation-assisted E0 estimation has also been run; the first-stage grouped-E0 checkpoint on its small grouped test split has force RMSEs of 19.6, 10.9, 13.7, 42.9, and 9.2 meV A^-1 for pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and the Si4--graphene motif, respectively. Because this grouped test set has only five held-out frames and high-displacement snapshot errors remain mixed, we still present it as a diagnostic audit rather than as final force-field validation.

We also added a three-seed committee diagnostic. The committee validation force RMSE values are 96.4, 107.2, and 48.4 meV A^-1. The manuscript uses this spread as a caution against overinterpreting individual finite-temperature trajectories.

## Comment 3: Wrapped-Coordinate MSD Artifact And Insufficient MD Statistics

We agree. The revised analysis replaces the wrapped-coordinate 10 ps trajectories with 15 unwrapped-coordinate 400 K NVT trajectories: five structures times three velocity seeds, 100 ps each. All 15 trajectories completed without LAMMPS errors, lost atoms, or NaNs. A follow-up GPU production set further completed 18/18 target-length 200--500 ps trajectories without LAMMPS errors, lost atoms, NaNs, or dangerous neighbor-list builds.

The revised manuscript reports these trajectories only as finite-window stability and displacement diagnostics. It no longer reports them as converged diffusion coefficients and no longer claims fast practical Li transport. The MD section also clarifies that the 2 x 2 x 1 replicated cells contain multiple Li atoms and periodic defect copies, so the trajectories are not concentration-converged electrode models.

## Comment 4: Possible MLIP Extrapolation In High-Displacement Trajectories

We agree that high-displacement configurations require direct DFT checks before they can be interpreted as physical transport or reconstruction mechanisms. The revised manuscript therefore removes claims that large-displacement trajectories prove fast Li transport, defect reconstruction, or a robust Si4--graphene diffusion mechanism.

Representative DFT snapshot checks have been prepared as follow-up validation, and nine high-displacement snapshots from the initial 100 ps campaign have now completed as usable spin-polarized DFT single-point checks. These include six Si4--graphene snapshots and three monovacancy snapshots, spanning MSDxy values from 207.6 to 2694.8 A^2. All nine current OUTCAR files reached electronic convergence, had no fatal markers, and have ASE-readable forces; an evidence archive records per-row OUTCAR SHA256 hashes. A simple geometry screen found no sub-A atom overlaps, although the monovacancy snapshots contain short C-C contacts of 1.204, 1.212, and 1.217 A and one Si4--graphene snapshot contains a short Si-Si contact of 1.994 A. Direct MACE-MPA-0-vs-DFT force errors are large on these rows. The grouped-E0 fine-tuned model improves the Si4--graphene snapshot force RMSE to 114 meV A^-1, but monovacancy remains poor at 1107 meV A^-1 and the all-snapshot grouped-E0 value is still 646 meV A^-1. The manuscript therefore frames these rows as out-of-domain stress tests rather than as validation of a diffusion mechanism or converged diffusion coefficient. Production-trajectory high-displacement snapshots are being checked separately and are not used for claims until complete.

## Comment 5: DFT Details, Structural Relaxation, And Literature Context

We expanded the Methods section to state the VASP setup, including PAW/PBE, spin polarization, ENCUT, k-point mesh, smearing, electronic and ionic convergence thresholds, LASPH, ADDGRID, and symmetry settings. The manuscript also explicitly states that the fixed-geometry scans do not include completed CI-NEB barriers. The MACE training labels did not include vdW or dipole corrections, so we added follow-up PBE-D3/dipole single-point adsorption-energy checks. All 11 adsorption-energy component jobs completed with electronic convergence and no fatal VASP marker; the resulting E_ads values are -0.634, -3.115, -1.328, 0.009, and -3.349 eV per Li for pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and the Si4--graphene motif, respectively. These values are reported only as single-geometry adsorption anchors, not as voltage, capacity, clustering, or migration-barrier claims.

The Introduction and Discussion now compare the study with prior DFT literature on Li adsorption and diffusion on graphene, vacancies, divacancies, Stone--Wales defects, Li clustering, and silicon-containing anode materials. The text emphasizes that the present Si-containing model is a Si4--graphene local motif, not a representative silicon--graphene composite anode.

## Comment 6: Overclaiming Battery-Anode Performance

We agree. The title, abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion have been rewritten to avoid claims about capacity, voltage, rate capability, cycling stability, practical anode performance, mechanical advantage, or electronic advantage. The revised manuscript states that a single-Li model on one graphene sheet is a dilute-limit local chemistry model and cannot establish realistic storage performance.

The conclusion now presents the transferable result as a validation-first evidence hierarchy: first-principles anchors establish local energetics, leakage-aware tests bound interpolation, and DFT stress tests identify the next training-data targets. It does not claim quantitative lithium diffusion or practical anode performance.

## Comment 7: Reproducibility

We expanded the workflow documentation and generated reproducibility materials for the revision. The project now contains VASP preparation scripts, MACE evaluation scripts, committee training scripts, LAMMPS unwrapped-MD inputs, analysis scripts, processed CSV files, generated figures, status summaries, an MIT license, and a reproducibility archive manifest. A compact submission-data package adds both extxyz split definitions, the 273-frame labels, curated evidence tables, and a SHA256 manifest while removing machine-specific absolute paths. Because the remote repository is currently private, the revised Data and Code Availability section no longer claims that it is publicly accessible. Instead, a version-pinned code-and-data archive is supplied as supplementary material for peer review, with licensed POTCARs and large VASP/LAMMPS runtime files excluded; a public release URL or DOI will be added before publication.
