Please consider our Research Article, "Validation-first machine learning
interatomic potentials for local lithium energetics in graphene-based battery
motifs: a DFT-MACE workflow," for publication in Computational Materials
Science.

This work presents a validation-first workflow for AI-enabled atomistic
materials modeling, demonstrated for dilute lithium in five graphene-based
environments spanning Li-C and Li-C-Si chemistry. Its central advance is
methodological:
each increase in simulation scale is paired with an explicit evidence gate.
The workflow combines spin-polarized DFT labeling, MACE-MPA-0 fine-tuning,
group-aware data splitting, atomic-reference calibration, committee
sensitivity, PBE-D3(BJ)/dipole adsorption anchors, finite-temperature molecular
dynamics, and direct DFT force tests of extrapolative snapshots.

The study shows why low random-split errors are insufficient for AI-for-science
and AI-enabled battery-materials workflows. In an initial same-workflow split,
target-domain fine-tuning reduces the force RMSE from 285.2 to 20.1 meV per
angstrom, but
the audit also exposes path-image leakage, an atomic-reference calibration
problem, and a high-displacement regime that remains outside the reliable force
domain. These failure modes define concrete next-label targets and restrict the
supported physical claims. The resulting contribution is therefore an evidence
hierarchy for deciding when a foundation-model potential supports local
screening and when additional first-principles data are required.

The manuscript fits the journal's interest in modern computational methods,
two-dimensional materials, machine-learning-enhanced simulation, and
reproducible data-driven workflows. This is not a conventional MD
average-property study: trajectories are used to expose extrapolative
configurations, which are then tested directly against DFT. The manuscript
deliberately excludes unsupported claims about migration barriers, converged
diffusion coefficients, or practical anode performance. A version-pinned code-and-data archive
is deposited on Zenodo under DOI
10.5281/zenodo.21609229, with curated data under CC BY 4.0 and workflow code
under MIT. The development repository is retained at
https://github.com/xiaohuang-6/Li-vasp and will be made public immediately
after manuscript submission. The same archive is supplied as supplementary
material for peer review.

Thank you for considering this manuscript.
