================ EDITOR / GENERAL RESPONSE ================

Response: Thank you for the opportunity to resubmit this work. We have
substantially revised the manuscript and addressed every numbered point raised
by Reviewers 2 and 3. The revised article has a new title and is organized around
the scientific question of how graphene defects and a local Si4 motif affect Li
adsorption and the local transferability of a fine-tuned foundation MLIP. The
Abstract, Introduction, Results and Discussion, Conclusion, figures, and Methods
have been rewritten or reorganized. The revision adds direct, family-balanced
DFT force validation, an explicit comparison with DeePMD-kit/DP-GEN, complete
MACE training and split details, and a dedicated Supporting Information section.
The submission also includes editable manuscript and figure source files,
Highlights, a Graphical Abstract, Supporting Information, and a version-pinned
code-and-data archive. Point-by-point responses and exact paragraph, page, and
line references are provided below.


================ REVIEWER 2 - COMMENT 1 ================
Relationship to concurrent-learning and active-learning workflows

Response: We agree. The revised Introduction now states that the
explore-label-retrain paradigm is established rather than novel. It describes
the DP-GEN sequence of exploration, ensemble model-deviation screening,
first-principles labeling, and retraining, and cites both DeePMD-kit and DP-GEN.
We then state the precise distinction: the present study does not use a
quantitative model-deviation threshold and does not complete a subsequent
retraining cycle. Direct DFT calculations instead delimit the supported
adsorption and force-transferability conclusions and identify targeted future
labels. Supporting Information Table 2 provides a six-row comparison of the
potential model, exploration method, selection signal, DFT step, retraining
loop, and purpose.

Changes in the revised manuscript: Introduction, paragraph 3, p. 2, lines
46-53; references 14 and 15, p. 13, lines 387-391; Supporting Information
Table 2, p. 2.


================ REVIEWER 2 - COMMENT 2 ================
Precision of the novelty statement

Response: We agree and have removed "validation-first" from the title. The

================ REVIEWER 2 - COMMENT 3 ================
Role of the three-member MACE committee

Response: The committee was used only to evaluate sensitivity to independent
fine-tuning seeds. No calibrated uncertainty estimate, committee-disagreement
threshold, or force-deviation threshold was used to select configurations for
DFT. Membership of the new five-family perturbation benchmark was also fixed
without MACE energies, forces, or committee disagreement. The manuscript now
states these points directly and does not describe the committee as an
uncertainty ensemble.

Changes in the revised manuscript: MACE potential development and validation,
paragraph 4, p. 6, lines 164-172; seed-resolved metrics remain in Supporting
Information Section 1.


================ REVIEWER 2 - COMMENT 4 ================
Limitations of the grouped split

Response: We agree. The five grouped test configurations are not used as a
population-level transferability estimate. The revised text treats this split
as a correlation diagnostic and retains the original correlated-split result
only as an interpolation baseline. We also added a post-training force
benchmark containing five predefined perturbations from each of the five
structural families, for 25 new DFT configurations in total. Configuration
selection used neither MACE predictions nor committee disagreement. All 25 DFT
single points converged electronically. Fine-tuning reduces the pooled force
RMSE from 450.0 to 267.5 meV A^-1 and lowers the family-resolved RMSE in every
environment by 35.4-43.2%. The frame-level force RMSE is lower for 23 of the 25
configurations. The manuscript still states explicitly that this benchmark
establishes local robustness around the five reference structures rather than
global transferability.

Changes in the revised manuscript: MACE potential development and validation,
paragraphs 1 and 5, pp. 5-6, lines 132-142 and 173-184; Figure 2, p. 8; Results
and Discussion, Section 3.3, paragraphs 1-4, pp. 9-10, lines 250-275;
family-resolved values in Supporting Information Table 4, p. 3; frame-level
values in the Supplementary Data archive. The 285.2-to-20.1 meV A^-1
correlated-split metric has been removed from the Abstract.

================ REVIEWER 2 - COMMENT 7 ================
Emphasis on direct DFT validation

Response: We agree. Direct DFT force comparisons are now central to Figure 2,
the Results and Discussion, and the Conclusion. The family-balanced test
evaluates predefined controlled local perturbations, while the separate
nine-frame comparison probes selected high-displacement monovacancy and
Si4-graphene configurations. Interpretation is tied to measured force errors
and, for the monovacancy subset, to short reconstructed C-C contacts. We do not
infer that all high-displacement configurations are outside the applicability
domain. On the nine-frame comparison, fine-tuning lowers the Si4-graphene force
RMSE from 310 to 113 meV A^-1 but changes the monovacancy value only from 1133
to 1097 meV A^-1, directly establishing the environment-resolved contrast.

================ REVIEWER 3 - COMMENT 4 ================
Complete the potential-development methodology

Response: We added a dedicated "MACE potential development and validation"
subsection. It reports the inherited architecture, cutoff, interaction layers,
irreducible representations, correlation order, angular limit, precision,
batch size, epochs, learning rate, optimizer, weight decay, exponential moving
average, stochastic-weight-averaging transition, stage weights, trainable
components, selected checkpoints, and all three E0 offsets. The architecture
was inherited unchanged rather than selected against the test set. The E0
estimate is a deterministic full-rank linear solve, not an iterative
optimization; acceptance requires finite coefficients and elemental rank 3/3.

The dataset is described as a deliberately compact sample of five local
chemical families and selected displacement coordinates, not exhaustive
configurational coverage. The exact correlation-controlled split algorithm and
the 263/5/5 outcome are stated. Supporting Information Tables 1-2 provide the
complete split metrics, seed sensitivity, E0 details, and workflow comparison.
The Methods also gives the complete selection and DFT protocol for the
independently defined 25-configuration benchmark and the finite-temperature
snapshot calculations.
