\begin{abstract}
Graphene defects can strongly alter Li binding, but the same local
reconstructions that create adsorption sites can also challenge the
transferability of machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs). We combine
spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) with the Message Passing Atomic
Cluster Expansion (MACE) architecture, starting from the MACE-MPA-0 foundation
model, to compare dilute Li adsorption and model
transferability across pristine, monovacancy, divacancy, and Stone--Wales
graphene and a local Si$_4$--graphene motif. PBE-D3(BJ) fixed-geometry single
points with a slab dipole correction give adsorption energies from -3.349 to
+0.009 eV per Li. Relative to pristine graphene (-0.634 eV), the sampled
monovacancy and Si$_4$ geometries strengthen Li adsorption by 2.481 and
2.715 eV, respectively. Fine-tuning reduces the force root-mean-square error
(RMSE) on a correlated same-workflow test set from 285.2 to
20.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$, showing strong interpolation improvement but not independent
transferability. Direct DFT force tests on selected high-displacement snapshots
reveal the chemically resolved behavior: corrected fine-tuning lowers the
Si$_4$--graphene snapshot force RMSE from 310 to 114 meV \AA$^{-1}$, whereas the
monovacancy error changes only from 1149 to 1107 meV \AA$^{-1}$. Thus, aggregate
test errors conceal a reconstructed-monovacancy regime that remains outside the
learned force domain. These results show that defect-dependent adsorption and
defect-dependent MLIP reliability must be evaluated together, and they identify
targeted monovacancy configurations as the priority for training-set expansion.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} machine-learned interatomic potentials; MACE; lithium adsorption; graphene defects; density functional theory; foundation models; transferability

\section{Introduction}

Defects in graphitic carbon create chemically distinct adsorption environments
for Li and can substantially change local binding relative to pristine graphene
\citep{uthaisar2010edge,zhou2012graphene,yildirim2014defect}. Vacancies expose
under-coordinated carbon atoms and permit local reconstruction, whereas a
Stone--Wales defect changes ring topology without removing carbon. Silicon adds
a different bonding environment and is widely studied in composite anodes,
although realistic Si-containing electrodes also involve alloying, volume
change, and heterogeneous microstructure \citep{obrovac2014alloy,sehrawat2021silicongraphene,palumbo2019siliconflg}.
Comparing these local environments at the dilute single-Li level provides a
controlled way to isolate how defect chemistry changes adsorption.

MLIPs can extend first-principles models toward larger configuration spaces and
finite-temperature sampling \citep{jacobs2025mlipguide}. MACE represents
higher-order atomic correlations through equivariant message passing
\citep{batatia2022mace}, and the MACE-MPA-0 foundation model provides a broad
starting point for materials chemistry \citep{batatia2025foundation}. Fine-tuning
can improve accuracy in a target domain \citep{radova2025finetuning}, but average
errors on configurations similar to the training data do not establish
reliability after defect reconstruction or large atomic displacement. Recent
studies have shown that training-set composition, foundation-model bias, and
potential-energy-surface softening can produce chemically specific
extrapolation failures \citep{niblett2025transferability,wong2026bias,deng2025softening}.

The explore--label--retrain logic is not unique to the present study. DeePMD-kit
and the DP-GEN concurrent-learning framework, for example, combine model
training, configuration-space exploration, ensemble-based model-deviation
screening, first-principles labeling, and iterative retraining
\citep{wang2018deepmd,zhang2020dpgen}. Our workflow shares the use of
finite-temperature exploration and committee diagnostics, but it does not apply
a numerical model-deviation threshold and does not close a further retraining
cycle. Its purpose here is narrower: independent DFT checks determine which
scientific claims about adsorption and local MLIP transfer are supported and
identify the next configurations that require labeling. We therefore treat this
as a validation-driven diagnostic, not as a conceptually new concurrent-learning
algorithm.

This issue is especially important for defective graphene. A potential may
describe intact or Si-containing environments while failing for short,
reconstructed C--C contacts generated near a vacancy. Such a failure can be
hidden by an aggregate test metric dominated by more familiar configurations.
The relevant scientific question is therefore twofold: how strongly does each
local environment bind Li, and does a fine-tuned foundation MLIP retain similar
force accuracy when those environments are displaced beyond the configurations
used for training?

Here we address this question for pristine, monovacancy, divacancy, and
Stone--Wales graphene and a minimal Si$_4$--graphene motif. We first establish
consistent PBE-D3(BJ) adsorption-energy anchors with a slab dipole correction.
We then compare the MACE-MPA-0 foundation model with target-domain fine-tuning
on the labeled workflow and, more stringently, against DFT forces for selected
high-displacement monovacancy and Si$_4$--graphene snapshots. The results reveal
both a broad adsorption-energy range and a strong local-environment dependence
of MLIP transfer: fine-tuning substantially improves the Si$_4$ subset but leaves
the reconstructed monovacancy subset at 1.1 eV \AA$^{-1}$ force
RMSE. This contrast defines a concrete, chemistry-based target for subsequent
training-data expansion.

\section{Computational methods}

\subsection{Model systems}

Five model systems were constructed with the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) \citep{larsen2017ase}: pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and a Si$_4$--graphene motif. A graphene lattice parameter of 2.46 \AA{} and a 5 $\times$ 5 supercell gave an intact C$_{50}$ sheet. The 30.0 \AA{} cell height places 15.0 \AA{} of vacuum on each side of the initial graphene plane. A monovacancy was generated by removing one central C atom, and a divacancy was generated by removing a nearest-neighbor C--C pair. The Stone--Wales starting geometry was generated by rotating a central C--C bond. The resulting initial formulas were C$_{50}$Li, C$_{49}$Li, C$_{48}$Li, C$_{50}$Li, and C$_{50}$LiSi$_4$, respectively.

The Si-containing system places four non-substitutional Si atoms above the intact C$_{50}$ sheet and is described as a Si$_4$--graphene motif, not as a general Si--graphene composite. This motif is a deliberately minimal local model: four Si atoms are sufficient to introduce Si--Si bonding and local Li--Si/Si--C interactions without attempting to represent the alloying, volume expansion, and heterogeneous microstructure of realistic Si--graphene anodes \citep{obrovac2014alloy,sehrawat2021silicongraphene}. Its stability is therefore a DFT relaxation and adsorption question rather than an assumption built into the model. A Li atom was placed near the central adsorption region of each structure. Figure~\ref{fig:structures} defines the model families.

\subsection{DFT labeling}

DFT calculations were performed with VASP 5.4.1 \citep{kresse1996efficient,kresse1996cms} using PAW potentials \citep{blochl1994paw,kresse1999paw} and the PBE exchange--correlation functional \citep{perdew1996pbe}. The archived OUTCAR headers identify the PAW\_PBE datasets as C (08Apr2002), Li\_sv (10Sep2004), and Si (05Jan2001). The calculations used spin polarization, ENCUT = 520 eV, a Gamma-centered 3 $\times$ 3 $\times$ 1 k-point mesh, Gaussian smearing with ISMEAR = 0 and SIGMA = 0.05 eV, PREC = Accurate, LREAL = Auto, ALGO = Normal, NELM = 120, LASPH = .TRUE., ADDGRID = .TRUE., and ISYM = 0. All ionic coordinates were relaxed without selective-dynamics constraints at fixed lattice vectors using IBRION = 2, ISIF = 2, NSW = 100, EDIFF = $10^{-5}$ eV, and EDIFFG = -0.02 eV \AA$^{-1}$. Single-point site and path-scan labels used IBRION = -1, NSW = 0, and EDIFF = $10^{-6}$ eV. The MACE training labels did not include an explicit vdW correction or slab dipole correction. This omission is important because Li adsorption on graphitic surfaces and one-sided slab adsorbates can be sensitive to both effects \citep{grimme2010d3}.

Additional adsorption checks therefore used fixed-geometry single points on the current Li+substrate and Li-removed substrate geometries with IBRION = -1, NSW = 0, EDIFF = $10^{-6}$ eV, LREAL = .FALSE., NELM = 180, and the same 3 $\times$ 3 $\times$ 1 slab k-point mesh. These calculations applied the DFT-D3 method with Becke--Johnson damping (PBE-D3(BJ); IVDW = 12) \citep{grimme2010d3,grimme2011bj} and a slab dipole correction (LDIPOL = .TRUE., IDIPOL = 3). A separate isolated Li atom reference used a 20 \AA{} cubic cell, a Gamma-only 1 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 1 mesh, and EDIFF = $10^{-7}$ eV. Adsorption energies are reported as
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ads}} = E(\mathrm{Li+substrate}) - E(\mathrm{substrate}) - n_{\mathrm{Li}}E(\mathrm{Li\ atom}).
\end{equation}
These checks are adsorption-energy anchors for the current geometries, not exhaustive relaxed adsorption-site searches.

The final MACE dataset contains 273 frames: 194 frames from ionic relaxation trajectories and 79 fixed-geometry site/path single-point calculations. Site labels sample the initial Li position, hollow-like sites, bridge-like sites, top-like sites, and Si$_4$-related positions. Path labels are linear Cartesian interpolation images between two sampled endpoints. This deliberately compact dataset samples five local chemical families and selected displacement coordinates; it is not intended to exhaust the configurational space of defective graphene or Si-containing electrodes. Independent displaced-configuration DFT checks therefore provide the transferability test rather than the dataset size alone. No completed climbing-image NEB result is used in the present analysis \citep{henkelman2000neb}. Consequently, the path quantities below are reported as fixed-geometry path-scan descriptors and endpoint energy differences, not as migration barriers.

\subsection{Potential development and validation workflow}

The DFT-labeled structures were converted to extended XYZ files. The first diagnostic split contained 211 training frames, 31 validation frames, and 31 held-out test frames, but this split mixed correlated images from the same fixed-geometry interpolation paths across train/validation/test. It is therefore retained only as an initial same-workflow diagnostic. The audited workflow keeps every relaxation trajectory in training, keeps all images from each fixed path in one group, sorts the remaining site/path groups within each structural family, and assigns those groups by a deterministic 8:1:1 train/validation/test cycle. In the present 273-frame dataset, this leakage-audited split contains 263 training frames, 5 validation frames, and 5 test frames. The validation and test sets each contain one configuration from each of the five families. These small held-out sets define a leakage audit rather than a complete transferability benchmark.

The MACE-MPA-0 medium foundation model \citep{batatia2025foundation} was fine-tuned from the checkpoint file \path{mace-mpa-0-medium.model}. Its inherited architecture used a 6.0 \AA{} radial cutoff, two interaction layers, hidden irreducible representations \(128\times 0e + 128\times 1o\), correlation order 3, and spherical harmonics through \(l=3\). This architecture was inherited unchanged rather than selected by screening the present test set. The first reference fine-tuned run, identified as \path{li_mace_v1_3060ti}, used seed 20260427 and was used for the same-workflow error table and the 500 ps reference trajectories. This MACE 0.3.15 run used double precision, batch size 2, 300 epochs, an initial learning rate of \(5\times 10^{-4}\), Adam/AMSGrad optimization with weight decay \(5\times 10^{-7}\), exponential moving-average decay 0.99, and stochastic weight averaging from epoch 225. First-stage energy and force weights were 1.0 and 100.0; the second-stage weights were 1000.0 and 100.0.

The three-seed committee and grouped-\(E_0\) audit runs used MACE 0.3.16, batch size 4, 300 epochs, an initial learning rate of \(10^{-3}\), and the same epoch-225 stochastic-weight-averaging transition. The committee runs used single precision, whereas the grouped-\(E_0\) run used double precision. In all fine-tuning runs, embedding and interaction learning-rate factors were zero, while product and readout factors were one. This learning-rate-factor strategy avoids hard-coding a layer index for freezing the foundation-model backbone. The fixed-length training selected epoch 224 for first-stage evaluation and epoch 298 for second-stage evaluation according to the validation metric recorded by the training script. Because the first run showed a large Si$_4$--graphene energy offset, the corrected training uses foundation-assisted \(E_0\) estimation rather than the original atomic-reference estimate. With \texttt{--E0s=estimated}, the archived log records foundation-model predictions over all 263 grouped training configurations, a full-rank (3/3) elemental correction fit, and final baseline offsets of -3.271318, -1.246324, and -1.280346 eV for Li, C, and Si, respectively. The \(E_0\) estimate is a deterministic linear solve, not an iterative optimization; acceptance required finite coefficients and full elemental rank. These are model energy offsets, not isolated-atom energies. Exact command-line settings and the archived grouped-\(E_0\) training log are included in the reproducibility package.

We report the original same-workflow errors as an interpolation baseline and use
the grouped split and corrected $E_0$ model for the high-displacement force
comparison. A direct comparison with the unfine-tuned MACE-MPA-0 foundation
model determines whether target-domain fine-tuning improves each selected local
environment. Three additional random-seed models quantify training sensitivity;
their detailed metrics are reported in the Supporting Information. The
three-member committee is used only as a qualitative seed-sensitivity diagnostic.
No numerical committee-disagreement or force-deviation threshold is used to
select configurations for DFT, so it should not be interpreted as a calibrated
uncertainty ensemble of the type used in concurrent-learning workflows.

\subsection{Finite-temperature snapshot generation}

LAMMPS (10 September 2025) \citep{plimpton1995lammps} simulations used the
converted MACE models with the MACE pair style. The five structures were
replicated 2 $\times$ 2 $\times$ 1, giving 196--220 atoms, and sampled at 400 K
with a 1 fs timestep after 10 ps of Nos\'e--Hoover NVT equilibration. These
finite-temperature trajectories were used to generate displaced configurations,
not to determine diffusion coefficients. Successful trajectory completion
establishes numerical execution only; it does not establish the physical
reliability of the sampled dynamics. Nine selected high-displacement
snapshots (three monovacancy and six Si$_4$--graphene) were evaluated directly
with spin-polarized DFT using the original labeling protocol and Gamma-only
sampling. Frame-level force RMSE was computed against both MACE-MPA-0 and the
grouped-$E_0$ fine-tuned model. Seven additional Si$_4$--graphene snapshots from
longer trajectories received converged DFT single-point checks. In total, 16
electronically converged DFT snapshot checks are reported
in the Supporting Information as coverage evidence; they are not included in
the nine-frame force-error comparison. None of these DFT-validated snapshots is
incorporated into a subsequent MACE training cycle in the present work. They
define targets for a future dataset-expansion and retraining iteration; a closed
active-learning cycle is beyond the scope of this study.

\section{Conclusion}

Defect chemistry changes both Li adsorption and MLIP reliability in the present
graphene-based environments. The sampled monovacancy and Si$_4$ geometries bind
Li 2.481 and 2.715 eV more strongly than pristine graphene, while the sampled
Stone--Wales geometry is 0.643 eV less favorable. Target-domain MACE fine-tuning
strongly improves forces within the sampled workflow and reduces the selected
Si$_4$ high-displacement force RMSE from 310 to 114 meV \AA$^{-1}$. The same
model, however, leaves the reconstructed-monovacancy error at
1107 meV \AA$^{-1}$.

The scientific implication is direct: an aggregate MLIP error does not define a
single transferability limit across chemically distinct defects. In this system,
strained monovacancy reconstruction is the priority environment for targeted
DFT labeling, whereas the Si$_4$ subset already benefits substantially from
fine-tuning. Broader environment-balanced snapshot tests and relaxed adsorption
searches are required to generalize the numerical values, but the current data
establish the local-environment dependence that should guide that expansion.
