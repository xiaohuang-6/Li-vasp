\begin{abstract}
Defects in graphene and local Si--C motifs can reshape dilute-limit Li binding and local energy landscapes, but reliable kinetic claims require adsorption energies, minimum-energy paths, and model validation that are separated from workflow artifacts. We present a reproducible DFT--MACE diagnostic workflow for five local Li--C--Si environments: pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and a Si$_4$--graphene motif. The DFT label set contains 273 spin-polarized VASP frames from ionic relaxations and fixed-geometry site/path scans. Initial MACE-MPA-0 fine-tuning reduced the same-workflow test force RMSE from 285.2 to 20.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$, demonstrating that the foundation model alone is inadequate for these local Li--C--Si configurations. However, a subsequent audit identified two issues that prevent treating the model as a final force field: correlated path images were mixed across the original split, and the Si$_4$--graphene energy RMSE of 108.3 meV atom$^{-1}$ indicates an atomic-reference/E0 calibration problem rather than a physical offset. The audited workflow therefore uses group-held-out train/validation/test splits and foundation-assisted E0 estimation. Additional PBE-D3/dipole single-point checks give adsorption energies from -3.35 to +0.01 eV per Li for the present dilute-limit geometries. Short 400 K LAMMPS trajectories are retained only as stability and displacement diagnostics; their MSD traces are not converted into diffusion coefficients. Nine high-displacement snapshots from the initial 100 ps campaign have OUTCAR/hash evidence for electronic convergence and readable DFT forces; direct MACE--DFT force comparison shows that such snapshots are a stringent out-of-domain test. The present study consequently supports local adsorption-energy screening and model-diagnostic conclusions, while CI-NEB barriers and converged diffusion statistics are excluded from the quantitative claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Lithium-ion batteries motivate continued study of carbon and silicon-containing negative-electrode materials, but atomistic calculations must distinguish local adsorption descriptors from electrochemical anode performance \citep{tarascon2001issues,obrovac2014alloy}. Graphite remains the commercial benchmark anode, whereas graphene and graphene-derived carbons offer excellent electronic conductivity and tunable defect chemistry that can modify local Li adsorption and migration behavior \citep{novoselov2004electric,yoo2008graphene}. A single-Li model on one graphene sheet is therefore best viewed as a dilute-limit local chemistry model. It cannot directly establish capacity, voltage, rate capability, cycle life, Li--Li clustering, multilayer transport, electrode--electrolyte effects, or Si alloying behavior.

The specific local problem considered here is already close to a substantial first-principles literature. Li adsorption and diffusion on graphene, graphene edges, vacancies, divacancies, and Stone--Wales defects have been examined with DFT and, in some cases, relaxed NEB calculations \citep{uthaisar2010edge,zhou2012graphene,yildirim2014defect}. These studies show that defects can strengthen Li binding and reshape local diffusion paths, but they also demonstrate why endpoint energy differences are not activation barriers. In pristine graphene, for example, the physically relevant hollow-to-hollow path passes through a bridge-like saddle region rather than being determined by endpoint energies alone \citep{uthaisar2010edge,zhou2012graphene}. First-principles studies of Li nucleation further caution that isolated Li adsorption on graphene is not automatically stable against clustering or metallic Li formation \citep{liu2014nucleation}. For Si-containing materials, realistic anode behavior additionally involves Li--Si alloying, large volume changes, and microstructural degradation \citep{obrovac2014alloy,sehrawat2021silicongraphene,palumbo2019siliconflg}. The Si$_4$--graphene motif used here is consequently a local compositional motif, not a full silicon--graphene composite anode model.

Machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) can make finite-temperature sampling more affordable once their training domain and uncertainty are controlled \citep{behler2016perspective,jacobs2025mlipguide}. MACE uses higher-order equivariant message passing to build accurate force fields \citep{batatia2022mace}, and MACE-MPA-0 provides a broadly trained foundation model for materials chemistry \citep{batatia2025foundation}. Fine-tuning such a model can be useful for a target chemical domain, especially when parts of the foundation representation are kept near their pretrained values \citep{radova2025finetuning}. Related Li--C MLIP work, including Gaussian approximation potentials for Li intercalation in carbon nanostructures, also shows that model validation must be targeted to the intended downstream physics \citep{fujikake2018gap}.

This study is organized around three concrete deliverables. First, it builds a transparent DFT label set for dilute Li near five graphene-based local environments and adds PBE-D3/dipole single-point adsorption-energy anchors for the current geometries. Second, it audits a MACE fine-tuning workflow, including data-split leakage, atomic-reference calibration, and foundation-model comparison. Third, it uses short unwrapped-coordinate MD and DFT snapshot checks to identify which finite-temperature configurations require additional first-principles validation. Quantitative kinetic and battery-performance claims are reserved for calculations that provide relaxed site searches, CI-NEB barriers, and converged diffusion statistics.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Model systems}

Five model systems were constructed with the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) \citep{larsen2017ase}: pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and a Si$_4$--graphene motif. The starting cell was a 5 $\times$ 5 graphene supercell with 15.0 \AA{} of vacuum normal to the sheet. A monovacancy was generated by removing one central C atom, and a divacancy was generated by removing a nearest-neighbor C--C pair. The Stone--Wales model was generated by rotating a central C--C bond in the initial graphene lattice. The Si-containing system is described as a Si$_4$--graphene motif, not as a general Si--graphene composite. This motif is a deliberately minimal local model: four Si atoms are sufficient to introduce Si--Si bonding and local Li--Si/Si--C interactions without attempting to represent the alloying, volume expansion, and heterogeneous microstructure of realistic Si--graphene anodes \citep{obrovac2014alloy,sehrawat2021silicongraphene}. Its stability is therefore a DFT relaxation and adsorption question rather than an assumption built into the model. A Li atom was placed near the central adsorption region of each structure. Figure~\ref{fig:structures} defines the model families.

\subsection{DFT labeling}

DFT calculations were performed with VASP \citep{kresse1996efficient,kresse1996cms} using PAW potentials \citep{blochl1994paw,kresse1999paw} and the PBE exchange--correlation functional \citep{perdew1996pbe}. The calculations used spin polarization, ENCUT = 520 eV, a Gamma-centered 3 $\times$ 3 $\times$ 1 k-point mesh, Gaussian smearing with ISMEAR = 0 and SIGMA = 0.05 eV, LASPH = .TRUE., ADDGRID = .TRUE., and ISYM = 0. Ionic relaxations used IBRION = 2, NSW = 100, EDIFF = $10^{-5}$ eV, and EDIFFG = -0.02 eV \AA$^{-1}$. Single-point site and path-scan labels used NSW = 0 and EDIFF = $10^{-6}$ eV. The MACE training labels did not include an explicit vdW correction or slab dipole correction. This omission is important because Li adsorption on graphitic surfaces and one-sided slab adsorbates can be sensitive to both effects \citep{grimme2010d3}.

Additional adsorption checks therefore used non-ionic single points on the current Li+substrate and Li-removed substrate geometries with IBRION = -1, NSW = 0, EDIFF = $10^{-6}$ eV, the same 3 $\times$ 3 $\times$ 1 slab k-point mesh, PBE-D3 dispersion (IVDW = 12), and a slab dipole correction (LDIPOL = .TRUE., IDIPOL = 3). A separate isolated Li atom reference used a 20 \AA{} cubic cell, a Gamma-only 1 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 1 mesh, and EDIFF = $10^{-7}$ eV. Adsorption energies are reported as
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{ads}} = E(\mathrm{Li+substrate}) - E(\mathrm{substrate}) - n_{\mathrm{Li}}E(\mathrm{Li\ atom}).
\end{equation}
These checks are adsorption-energy anchors for the current geometries, not exhaustive relaxed adsorption-site searches.

The final MACE dataset contains 273 frames: 194 frames from ionic relaxation trajectories and 79 fixed-geometry site/path single-point calculations. Site labels sample the initial Li position, hollow-like sites, bridge-like sites, top-like sites, and Si$_4$-related positions. Path labels are linear Cartesian interpolation images between two sampled endpoints. No completed climbing-image NEB result is used in the present analysis \citep{henkelman2000neb}. Consequently, the path quantities below are reported as fixed-geometry path-scan descriptors and endpoint energy differences, not as migration barriers.

\subsection{MACE fine-tuning and validation}

The DFT-labeled structures were converted to extended XYZ files. The first diagnostic split contained 211 training frames, 31 validation frames, and 31 held-out test frames, but this split mixed correlated images from the same fixed-geometry interpolation paths across train/validation/test. It is therefore retained only as an initial same-workflow diagnostic. The audited data workflow groups all frames from a relaxation trajectory and all images from a fixed path before assigning them to a split; in the present 273-frame dataset this leakage-audited split contains 263 training frames, 5 validation frames, and 5 test frames. The small validation/test counts reflect the limited number of independent structure/path groups and define a leakage audit rather than a complete transferability benchmark.

The MACE-MPA-0 medium foundation model \citep{batatia2025foundation} was fine-tuned from the checkpoint file \path{mace-mpa-0-medium.model}. The first reference fine-tuned model, archived as \path{li_mace_v1_3060ti}, used seed 20260427 and was used for the same-workflow error table and the 500 ps reference trajectories. Training used a force-heavy objective with energy and force weights of 1.0 and 100.0. Embedding and interaction learning-rate factors were set to zero, while product and readout layers remained trainable; stochastic weight averaging was used in the second stage. This learning-rate-factor strategy avoids hard-coding a layer index for freezing the foundation-model backbone. Because the first run showed a large Si$_4$--graphene energy offset, the revision training uses foundation-assisted E0 estimation rather than a simple average atomic-energy reference.

We report the original same-workflow errors as a baseline diagnostic and use the leakage-audited split to define the corrected retraining workflow. A direct comparison with the unfine-tuned MACE-MPA-0 foundation model is retained because it answers a narrower, still valid question: whether the foundation potential alone is adequate for the present Li--C--Si labels. A three-member committee was also trained with different random seeds to estimate the sensitivity of the small-data fine-tuning procedure. Committee values taken from MACE training logs are identified as internal log metrics, whereas Table~\ref{tab:mace_errors} uses an external frame-RMS evaluator. The corrected E0/group-split training run is now treated as a diagnostic model-quality audit rather than a final force-field validation, because the grouped test split is very small and the direct high-displacement snapshot errors remain mixed.

\subsection{Short molecular dynamics diagnostics}

LAMMPS \citep{plimpton1995lammps} simulations used the converted MACE model with the MACE pair style. The small structures were replicated 2 $\times$ 2 $\times$ 1 for short MD diagnostics, producing 196--220 atoms depending on defect family. This replication also replicates Li and defects; for example, the pristine 2 $\times$ 2 $\times$ 1 cell contains four Li atoms, not one Li atom. This artificial periodic concentration must be considered when interpreting any Li displacement.

The initial molecular dynamics set was run at 400 K for 100 ps with a 1 fs timestep and three independent velocity seeds for each structure. A follow-up GPU production set extended the reference fine-tuned model to 500 ps for pristine graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and Si$_4$--graphene using three velocity seeds, and tested committee-model sensitivity for monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, and Si$_4$--graphene over 200 ps using three fine-tuning seeds. Coordinates were dumped with unwrapped Li positions or image-flag-consistent quantities, and Li in-plane MSD traces were post-processed from the unwrapped coordinates. We report these trajectories as finite-window stability and displacement diagnostics only. They are not long enough to produce converged diffusion coefficients, and the replicated small cells are not a concentration-converged model of a realistic electrode. To test whether the largest-displacement trajectories leave the DFT-labeled domain, representative high-displacement snapshots were prepared for spin-polarized VASP single-point checks using Gamma-only $1 \times 1 \times 1$ sampling. These checks are used only as qualitative MLIP-domain sanity tests.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Training diagnostics and held-out errors}

The dataset spans five distinct defect and compositional environments (Fig.~\ref{fig:mace_error}a). In the initial same-workflow split, the fine-tuned model reduced the all-family test force RMSE from 285.2 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the unfine-tuned MACE-MPA-0 foundation model to 20.1 meV \AA$^{-1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mace_error}b and Table~\ref{tab:mace_errors}). This force-error reduction demonstrates that fine-tuning is necessary for the present Li--C--Si labels. This value is not an independent transferability result because the split audit found correlated path images across data partitions. The energy diagnostics are also not acceptable for final thermodynamic claims: the Si$_4$--graphene test-family energy RMSE of 108.3 meV atom$^{-1}$ is most consistent with an atomic-reference/E0 calibration problem, not with a physically meaningful family offset. The corrected grouped-E0 run addresses this calibration route as a workflow audit, but its small held-out split and mixed high-displacement snapshot errors still prevent presenting the model as a final force field.

The three-member fine-tuning committee also completed successfully. The MACE training logs report internal validation force metrics of 96.4, 107.2, and 48.4 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for seeds 20260427, 20260428, and 20260429, respectively. These values are not directly combined with the external frame-RMS evaluator values in Table~\ref{tab:mace_errors}; they are used only as a seed-sensitivity warning for the small dataset. A grouped-split, foundation-assisted-E0 retraining run also completed. Its small grouped test split gives family force RMSEs of 19.6, 10.9, 13.7, 42.9, and 9.2 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for pristine graphene, monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and the Si$_4$--graphene motif, respectively. The fine-tuned potentials are clearly better than the foundation baseline for this target domain, but individual finite-temperature trajectories are interpreted with model-coverage caution. These values support interpolation within the present labeled structures but do not validate high Li concentration, Li clustering, or real Si--graphene composite lithiation.

\subsection{Fixed-geometry site energetics}

The fixed-geometry site scan identifies relative energies among the sampled Li placements (Fig.~\ref{fig:site_energy_scan}). Pristine graphene favors the hollow C$_3$ site among the sampled positions. Monovacancy, divacancy, and Si$_4$--graphene motifs have the initial Li position as the lowest sampled site. We do not use this result as evidence for a fully relaxed site preference: a ``prior Li'' minimum can indicate a real local trap, but it can also indicate that the search grid is too sparse or that the initial placement biased the sampled site set. In the Stone--Wales structure, the top-C site is lowest within this fixed-geometry scan; because prior literature emphasizes the importance of relaxed defect geometry, this result remains a fixed-geometry descriptor until relaxed adsorption-site searches are completed \citep{zhou2012graphene,yildirim2014defect}.

We report these numbers only as single-geometry adsorption energies, not as voltage, capacity, or clustering predictions.

The current data do not establish whether these local descriptors improve reversible storage. Defect- or Si-containing binding is therefore reported as a local adsorption descriptor, not as evidence for improved electrode performance.

\subsection{Endpoint energy differences, not migration barriers}

Table~\ref{tab:path_scans} and Fig.~\ref{fig:path_profiles} report fixed-geometry path-span descriptors rather than migration barriers. For several representative rows, the path-span values equal $|\Delta E_{\mathrm{end-start}}|$, which means the path maximum and minimum occur at endpoints. Such paths do not sample an independent saddle point. They are endpoint energy differences or fixed-geometry path spans, not activation barriers.

This correction changes the scientific interpretation. The 0.040 eV pristine value and 0.020 eV Stone--Wales value cannot be used to claim low Li migration barriers. They are small endpoint downhill energies in the sampled scans. A physically meaningful comparison with the graphene literature would require relaxed hollow--bridge--hollow or equivalent minimum-energy paths, preferably via CI-NEB \citep{uthaisar2010edge,zhou2012graphene,henkelman2000neb}. The present study therefore only discusses fixed-geometry local energy roughness.

\subsection{Short MD stability diagnostics}

The 400 K NVT trajectories are useful as model diagnostics but not as equilibrium diffusion simulations. The initial set comprised 15 trajectories of 100 ps each (five systems $\times$ three velocity seeds), and all completed without LAMMPS errors, lost atoms, or NaNs. The follow-up GPU production set further completed 18/18 target-length trajectories without LAMMPS errors, lost atoms, NaNs, or dangerous neighbor-list builds (Table~\ref{tab:md_diagnostics}). These runs include 500 ps reference-model trajectories for pristine graphene, Stone--Wales graphene, and Si$_4$--graphene, and 200 ps committee-model trajectories for monovacancy graphene, divacancy graphene, and Si$_4$--graphene. Across the follow-up set, final MSD$_{xy}$ values span 1.4--2436.1 \AA$^2$; for the Si$_4$--graphene motif they span 13.6--2436.1 \AA$^2$ in the three 500 ps reference-model runs and 110.4--1963.4 \AA$^2$ in the three 200 ps committee-model runs (Fig.~\ref{fig:md_diag}).

The key interpretation is conservative. Stable program completion and unwrapped-coordinate MSD traces show that the fine-tuned model can run finite-window trajectories across all five structural families. However, the Si$_4$--graphene reference-model final MSD$_{xy}$ values differ by 2422.5 \AA$^2$ across three velocity seeds, and the committee-model Si$_4$--graphene final MSD$_{xy}$ values differ by 1853.0 \AA$^2$ across three model seeds. These spreads make short-window slopes unsuitable as transport coefficients. We therefore report these data as stability and displacement diagnostics, not as final Li diffusivities or mechanistic proof of fast Si$_4$--graphene transport.

Nine DFT checks have now completed for selected high-displacement MACE snapshots from the initial 100 ps trajectory campaign (Table~\ref{tab:snapshot_dft_checks}). These rows are deliberately not presented as validation of the later 200--500 ps production set, which includes larger Si$_4$--graphene excursions than are covered by the table. The nine completed rows include six Si$_4$--graphene snapshots from two high-displacement initial Si$_4$ runs and three monovacancy snapshots from the highest-MSD initial monovacancy run. For each row, the revision archive records the OUTCAR SHA256 hash, final VASP energy, electronic-convergence marker, fatal-error screen, and ASE force-readability status. All nine current OUTCAR files are electronically converged and have readable DFT forces. Direct force comparison against the unfine-tuned MACE-MPA-0 foundation model gives a frame-level force RMSE of 710 meV \AA$^{-1}$ across the nine snapshots, including 1149 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the three monovacancy snapshots and 310 meV \AA$^{-1}$ for the six Si$_4$--graphene snapshots. The grouped-E0 fine-tuned model improves the Si$_4$--graphene snapshot force RMSE to 114 meV \AA$^{-1}$, but the monovacancy snapshot force RMSE remains 1107 meV \AA$^{-1}$ and the all-snapshot value remains 646 meV \AA$^{-1}$. These mixed errors confirm that high-displacement MD frames are a stringent out-of-domain validation set: the Si$_4$--graphene subset is improved by the corrected training route, but the full high-displacement set still requires active-learning-style expansion before mechanistic claims are made.

The monovacancy snapshots contain minimum C--C contacts of 1.204, 1.212, and 1.217 \AA{}, and one Si$_4$--graphene snapshot contains a minimum Si--Si contact of 1.994 \AA{}. Overall, the DFT calculations and force-error analysis change the conclusion from ``the jobs converged'' to a stronger diagnostic statement: large-displacement configurations can be parsed by DFT, but they lie far from the foundation-model force domain and must be included in model validation or active learning before mechanistic claims are made.

\subsection{Implications and required follow-up}

The revised evidence supports a more precise conclusion: defects and the local Si$_4$ motif produce large changes in sampled Li site energetics, and MACE fine-tuning is necessary before finite-temperature Li--C--Si simulations are attempted. At the same time, the data audit identifies the exact calculations required before restoring kinetic claims. We do not claim Li mobility, diffusion coefficients, rate capability, storage capacity, or mechanical/electronic advantages of Si--graphene anodes.

A stronger kinetic study requires specific additional calculations beyond the present evidence. First, the completed PBE-D3/dipole single-point adsorption anchors need full relaxed adsorption-site searches and sensitivity checks if adsorption thermodynamics become a central claim. Second, relaxed CI-NEB paths are required before any migration-barrier language is restored. Third, representative production-trajectory high-displacement DFT snapshots are required before interpreting the larger 200--500 ps excursions. Finally, longer trajectories and larger cells are required before converting the present short-window displacement diagnostics into quantitative diffusivity estimates.

\section{Conclusion}

This Li--MACE graphene study provides a leakage-audited local-energy and model-validation workflow. The DFT dataset documents sampled Li site energetics and shows that MACE-MPA-0 requires target-domain fine-tuning for Li--C--Si configurations. The three-seed committee, corrected grouped-E0 diagnostic run, initial set of 15 unwrapped-coordinate 100 ps MD diagnostics, follow-up 18-run 200--500 ps MD production set, PBE-D3/dipole adsorption-energy anchors, and nine DFT-converged high-displacement snapshot checks strengthen the diagnostic workflow. However, the grouped held-out split remains very small and high-displacement monovacancy snapshot errors remain large. The fine-tuned model is therefore not presented as a final force field. The fixed-geometry path scans are not migration barriers, and the finite-window MSD diagnostics are not converged diffusion coefficients. The Si-containing model is treated as a Si$_4$--graphene local motif rather than a representative silicon--graphene composite anode.

Overall, the workflow separates local adsorption-energy screening, force-field validation, and kinetic claims. Quantitative lithium diffusion or battery-performance claims require relaxed adsorption-site searches, relaxed CI-NEB barriers benchmarked against prior DFT work, representative production-snapshot DFT checks, and longer/larger-cell MD statistics.
