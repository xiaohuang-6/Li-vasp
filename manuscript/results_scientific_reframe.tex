\subsection{Defect-dependent Li adsorption}

\linelabel{r2:adsorption-start}Li binding differs substantially among the five structures
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}a). Across the 15 PBE-D3(BJ)
fixed-geometry calculations, the adsorption energies span $-2.967$ to $-0.875$ eV per Li.
The best sampled values are $-0.918$, $-2.967$, $-2.301$, $-1.587$, and $-2.465$ eV for
pristine, monovacancy, divacancy, Stone--Wales, and Si$_4$--graphene,
respectively. Thus, the monovacancy gives the strongest sampled adsorption,
followed by the Si$_4$ motif and divacancy.

The family trend is robust to the tested placement. All 12 non-pristine
energies are more negative than all three pristine energies. Even the least
favorable non-pristine placement, Stone--Wales at $-1.474$ eV, is 0.555 eV
more favorable than the best pristine placement at $-0.918$ eV. PBE-D3(BJ) also
retains the preceding PBE rank order of the three sites in every family. The
within-family spans range from 0.044 eV for pristine graphene to 0.906 eV for
the monovacancy, so absolute adsorption strengths remain site-sensitive even
though the defect-versus-pristine separation is unchanged.

The strong monovacancy binding is consistent with adsorption near
under-coordinated and reconstructed carbon, while the Si$_4$
motif introduces direct Li--Si and Si--C environments absent from pristine
graphene. The intermediate divacancy response and the stronger binding on
Stone--Wales graphene relative to pristine graphene show that both vacancy
structure and ring topology distinguish the adsorption environments.
Table~\ref{tab:si_d3_sites} gives the energy of each placement in the
fixed-geometry scan.

\subsection{Interpolation errors and the grouped split}
\label{sec:interpolation}

The 273-frame DFT label set spans all five structural families. On the original
correlated test split, the MACE-MPA-0 foundation model gives an all-family
force RMSE of 285.2 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}, whereas the first fine-tuned model gives
20.1 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}. Thus, target-domain fine-tuning reduces the force error by
92.9\% for configurations drawn from the same relaxation and site/path sampling
domain.

Table~\ref{tab:si_mace_errors} resolves this interpolation comparison by family
and partition. The Si$_4$ family has the largest energy error despite its small
force error, which motivated the separate correction of the elemental energy
offsets. The grouped comparison combines intact path groups with
foundation-assisted $E_0$ estimation to assess sensitivity to these two aspects
of model development. The one-configuration-per-family test set provides a
leakage audit, with broader local validation supplied by the independent
perturbation benchmark below.

\subsection{Force accuracy around the relaxed structures}

The 25-configuration benchmark tests five independently selected perturbations
per family
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}b). All 25 PBE single-point calculations
converged electronically. Across the complete benchmark, the pooled
force RMSE decreases from 450.0 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for MACE-MPA-0 to
267.5 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} after grouped-$E_0$ fine-tuning, a 40.6\% reduction. The
family-resolved force RMSE decreases in every environment: from 484.7 to 275.4
\si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for pristine graphene, 449.7 to 256.3 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for the
monovacancy, 381.3 to 246.3 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for the divacancy, 496.3 to 297.5
\si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for Stone--Wales graphene, and 427.5 to 258.5 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for
the Si$_4$ motif. At the individual-configuration level, the fine-tuned force
RMSE is lower for 23 of 25 predefined perturbations.
Table~\ref{tab:si_balanced_force} reports the family values and reductions.

\input{evidence_balanced_force}

The component-resolved comparison localizes the remaining error. Fine-tuning
reduces the Li-force RMSE from 992.6 to 65.1 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}, whereas the
substrate-force RMSE changes from 432.3 to 269.9 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}. The residual
balanced-test error is therefore dominated by forces on the substrate network.
The energy RMSE also decreases, from 26.6 to 4.6 \(\mathrm{meV}\,\mathrm{atom}^{-1}\).

Equal representation of the five families and selection independent of model
predictions make this benchmark a direct comparison of local force accuracy.
The family errors of 246--297 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} and the largest
frame error of 484 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} quantify performance over
the prescribed perturbations around the relaxed structures.

Resolving the errors by displacement amplitude shows where fine-tuning is most effective
(Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}c). For
coordinate perturbation standard deviations of 0.03, 0.06, and 0.10 \AA{}, the
fine-tuned pooled force RMSE is 79.2, 190.1, and 373.6 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom},
respectively. The corresponding MACE-MPA-0 values are 475.8, 435.6, and
450.8 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}, so the relative reduction narrows from 83.4\% near the
relaxed structures to 17.1\% at the largest perturbation. Fine-tuning therefore
improves every sampled family, with its largest gains close to the relaxed
reference structures.

The nine selected finite-temperature snapshots provide an explicit test outside
the small-displacement training domain (Fig.~\ref{fig:scientific_summary}d).
For the unfine-tuned foundation model, the frame-level force RMSE is
1133 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for the three monovacancy snapshots and
310 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} for the six Si$_4$--graphene snapshots. The grouped-$E_0$
fine-tuned model reduces the Si$_4$ error to 113 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}, a 63\%
reduction, but changes the monovacancy error only to
1097 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}, a 3\% reduction. Across all nine frames, the corresponding
RMSE changes from 701 to 640 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom}.

Fine-tuning improves the selected Si-containing configurations substantially,
while the monovacancy errors remain large. The
monovacancy snapshots contain minimum C--C distances of 1.204--1.217 \AA{}, much
shorter than an equilibrium graphitic C--C bond, whereas the selected Si$_4$
snapshots retain minimum C--C distances near 1.36 \AA{} and include Si--Si
contacts down to 1.994 \AA{}. The large monovacancy force errors therefore align
with a specific structural regime: highly strained local carbon reconstruction.
The combined force and contact-distance analysis localizes the observed
discrepancy to these strained carbon configurations.

Aggregate interpolation metrics would obscure this difference. The
correlated-split 20.1 \si{\milli\electronvolt\per\angstrom} force RMSE is much smaller than the
high-displacement errors, yet the two families respond
very differently to corrected fine-tuning. Additional training data should
include DFT configurations that resolve strained C--C
reconstruction and Li coordination near monovacancies, together with
family-balanced displaced configurations that test whether the observed
contrast persists beyond the present nine frames.

\subsection{Implications for potential development}

The combined analysis connects three levels of evidence to distinct material
properties. DFT adsorption energies resolve the effect of defect environment
on Li binding. The balanced perturbation benchmark quantifies the local
accuracy gained through fine-tuning, and species-resolved errors identify the
substrate network as the principal contributor to the residual force error.
Finite-temperature snapshots then distinguish the response of strained
monovacancy carbon from that of the sampled Si-containing environments.

This configuration-resolved interpretation is the methodological contribution
of the workflow: validation determines which local energetic and dynamical
properties the model describes and translates the observed force discrepancies
into specific targets for DFT labeling. It complements the automated
exploration and retraining of concurrent-learning methods by supplying a
physical interpretation of the validation results. For the present graphene
systems, that interpretation prioritizes strained carbon reconstruction and
Li coordination near vacancies alongside the family-balanced perturbations
that already demonstrate consistent gains from fine-tuning.
