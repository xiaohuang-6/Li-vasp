Please consider our Research Article, "Validation-first DFT-MACE screening of
local lithium energetics in defective graphene and Si4-graphene motifs," for
publication in Computational Materials Science.

This work presents a validation-first workflow for AI-enabled atomistic
materials modeling, demonstrated for dilute lithium in five graphene-based
Li-C-Si local environments. Its central advance is methodological:
each increase in simulation scale is paired with an explicit evidence gate.
The workflow combines spin-polarized DFT labeling, MACE-MPA-0 fine-tuning,
group-aware data splitting, atomic-reference calibration, committee
sensitivity, PBE-D3(BJ)/dipole adsorption anchors, finite-temperature molecular
dynamics, and direct DFT force tests of extrapolative snapshots.

The study shows why low random-split errors are insufficient for AI-for-science
and AI-enabled battery-materials workflows. Target-domain fine-tuning
reduces the same-workflow force RMSE from 285.2 to 20.1 meV/A, but the audit
also exposes path-image leakage, an atomic-reference offset, and a
high-displacement regime that remains outside the reliable force domain. These
failure modes define concrete next-label targets and restrict the supported
physical claims. The resulting contribution is therefore an evidence hierarchy
for deciding when a foundation-model potential supports local screening and
when additional first-principles data are required.

The manuscript fits the journal's interest in modern computational methods,
two-dimensional materials, machine-learning-enhanced simulation, and
reproducible data-driven workflows. It deliberately excludes unsupported
claims about migration barriers, converged diffusion coefficients, or
practical anode performance. The final submission will cite a public versioned
release or DOI-bearing repository record. A version-pinned code-and-data archive
will also be supplied as supplementary material for peer review.

Thank you for considering this manuscript.
