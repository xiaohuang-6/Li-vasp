Dear Dr. Liu,

Please consider our substantially revised Research Article,
"Local-environment-dependent transferability of a fine-tuned MACE potential for
lithium adsorption on graphene defects," for publication in Computational
Materials Science.

We thank you and the reviewers for identifying that the original submission did
not present its scientific contribution clearly enough. We have reorganized the
manuscript around a direct materials question: how graphene defects and a local
Si4 motif alter dilute Li adsorption, and how those chemically distinct
environments affect the transferability of a fine-tuned foundation MLIP.

The revised manuscript now leads with the first-principles findings. Consistent
PBE-D3(BJ) fixed-geometry calculations with a slab dipole correction show that
the sampled adsorption energies span -3.349 to +0.009 eV per Li. The
monovacancy and Si4 local geometries bind Li by 2.481 and 2.715 eV more strongly,
respectively, than the sampled pristine geometry. Direct DFT force tests on
high-displacement snapshots then reveal a local-environment-dependent MLIP
response: corrected fine-tuning reduces the Si4 snapshot force RMSE from 310 to
114 meV A^-1, while the monovacancy error remains above 1.1 eV A^-1. This
contrast identifies the reconstructed monovacancy environment as the priority
for targeted training-data expansion.

We have moved implementation-level split audits, calibration details, path
scans, runtime-completion logs, and full traceability tables out of the central
scientific narrative. The revised paper does not claim migration barriers,
converged diffusivities, or practical battery performance. A separate response
file addresses every reviewer comment and identifies the corresponding revised
lines.

The code and curated data are available in the version-pinned code-and-data archive
under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21609229 and at
https://github.com/xiaohuang-6/Li-vasp. Both authors contributed equally to this
work.

Thank you for considering the revised manuscript.
